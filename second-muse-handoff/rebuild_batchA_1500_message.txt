## 5. Term selection (corrected rule)

A term is a domain keyword or concept that carries meaning in THIS person's
work — a word or phrase the person uses to name a real idea, method,
product, or distinction in their content. The term MUST be a content word
(noun, noun phrase, or domain verb) that names something specific.

It must NOT be:

- A filler / stopword: way, gonna, like, just, really, know, want, get,
  make, people, video, thing, one, right, yeah, need, see, look, say,
  time, etc. — UNLESS the person uses it with a specific, distinctive
  meaning in this source, AND the Term paragraph shows that specific meaning.
- A person's name — UNLESS the name IS the concept (e.g. a named
  method/product named after a person), in which case document it as such.
- A repeated chant or slogan fragment ("money money", "THANK YOU",
  "Coca-Cola Coca-Cola").
- A generic word (design, content, video, step, number, speaker, swipe)
  UNLESS the person uses it with a specific, distinctive meaning in this
  source (and the Term paragraph must show that specific meaning).
- A word in a different script/language than the batch language (see §9).

CHANT RULE (N1) — pilot verdict 41/50 clean, 9 REJECT (7 of the 9 rejects
picked chanted lyric hooks as terms, e.g. "put your back into it",
"rhythm of the night", "all the way up", "do your thing", "patty cake"):
a chanted lyric hook / song refrain is NEVER the term, even when it is the
source's central phrase. A refrain is sung, not used as a concept — it names
no idea, method, product, or distinction. If the source is a pure chant
with no real domain term, use the honest-caption treatment: document the
chant/slogan as what it is and state plainly in the Term paragraph that the
source has no conceptual term — no term claim. Only pick a term when a real
content word exists in the source.

Selection method: READ the source, identify the 2–3 most meaningful content
keywords, pick the one that best names a real idea in this source.
Frequency is a TIEBREAKER, not the primary criterion. If two candidates are
close, prefer the more specific / domain-relevant one.

Short or noisy sources: if the source is very short (<100 words) or noisy
(chant, music, slogan), pick the source's CENTRAL CONCEPT — what the source
is ABOUT — even if it occurs only once or twice. Do NOT fall back to "most
frequent word" — that is what produced the junk. If the source has NO
meaningful concept (pure music/slogan with no content), use the honest-caption
treatment (see N1 above): document the chant/slogan as what it is and state plainly in the Term
paragraph that the source is a chant/slogan with no conceptual term — no term claim. Do NOT present
the central phrase as the term. Do not invent a term.

The term MUST occur literally in the source. This rule stays.

## 6. Term paragraph (corrected requirements)

Write a REAL paragraph (3–5 sentences) documenting how THIS person uses the
term in THIS source. It must:

- State what the term MEANS in this source (in the person's own usage, not a
  dictionary definition).
- Give the literal occurrence count (verified from the source — recount it).
- Give 2–4 collocated words/phrases (verified from the source).
- Give ONE concrete example of how the person uses it (a short paraphrase of
  a live example, or a description of the context — NOT a copy of the live
  example itself, which belongs in the Live Example section).
- Be written in FRESH WORDS for THIS term and THIS source.

It must be specific enough that a reader can tell what the term means in this
source without reading the live examples. If the paragraph could apply to any
source with the term swapped, it is boilerplate — rewrite it.

BANNED SENTENCES — the verification will reject any file containing them
(audit patterns A/B AND the batch-3 variant wording):

- "is the most frequent repeated content term in this source"
- "is the most prominent repeated term in this source"
- "No content word repeats often enough in this short source"
- "no term repeats enough for a frequency-based entry"
- "This entry documents usage in this source only and does not turn the term into a general definition outside it."
- "This entry documents usage in this specific source only and does not turn the term into a general definition outside it."
- "The live examples below are taken verbatim from the source and show its actual use in context."
- "The live examples below are taken verbatim from the transcript and show the term in its actual context."
